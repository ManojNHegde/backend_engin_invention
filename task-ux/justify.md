# Responsibility and Task: Definitions with Examples from Three Different Roles

## 1. Responsibility

**Definition:** The business result that a group of tasks exists to achieve. It is something the role must keep right and be accountable for if neglected. It is expressed as an outcome, not an activity.

### Example 1 — Credit Controller

**Responsibility:** Govern customer credit risk and credit-release decisions.

- **Outcome:** Customers receive the right credit decision, and good customers are not unnecessarily held up.
- **Accountability:** If risky orders are approved or good customers remain on hold unnecessarily, the responsibility is not being maintained.
- **Why it is a responsibility:** Multiple tasks, such as reviewing orders, placing credit holds, and requesting hold releases, contribute to this shared result.

### Example 2 — Payroll Specialist

**Responsibility:** Pay every employee the right amount, on time.

- **Outcome:** Employees receive the correct salary on payday.
- **Accountability:** If employees are paid incorrectly or late, the responsibility is not being maintained.
- **Why it is a responsibility:** Multiple tasks, such as applying approved salary changes, reviewing attendance, preparing payroll, and confirming payments, contribute to this shared result.

## 2. Task

**Definition:** A piece of work owned by one role that is worth calling done on its own. It has an identifiable start and end, serves a specific result, and has an observable completion outcome.

### Example 1 — Credit Controller

**Task T1:** Decide an order sent for credit review.

| Definition requirement | How T1 satisfies it |
|---|---|
| Owned by one role | The Credit Controller makes the credit decision. |
| Clear start | A Sales Order is routed for credit review. |
| Specific result | One order receives a credit decision. |
| Clear end | The decision and required reason are recorded on the order. |
| Observable completion | Someone can check the order and see the recorded decision. |
| Done independently | One order can be decided while other orders remain pending. |

**Conclusion:** T1 qualifies as one task because it has its own trigger, owner, result, and verifiable completion. Reading exposure, checking overdue payments, and recording the reason are steps within that task—not separate tasks.

### Example 2 — Payroll Specialist

**Task T1:** Plan the payroll month.

| Definition requirement | How T1 satisfies it |
|---|---|
| Owned by one role | The Payroll Specialist coordinates the payroll calendar. |
| Clear start | It starts 10 days before the month begins. |
| Specific result | Payroll dates are agreed upon and communicated. |
| Clear end | The Finance Controller agrees to the dates, and they are shared with HR, managers, Finance, and Accounting. |
| Observable completion | The agreed dates and communication can be checked. |
| Done independently | The calendar can be completed independently of calculating or processing salaries. |

**Conclusion:** T1 qualifies as one task because it has an identifiable start, a specific result, and verifiable completion. Agreeing on dates and communicating them are steps within the task—not separate tasks.

### Example 3 — Procurement Officer

**Task T2:** Raise a Purchase Order (PO).

| Definition requirement | How T2 satisfies it |
|---|---|
| Owned by one role | The Procurement Officer prepares and raises the PO. |
| Clear start | A need to create a PO arises, such as from a suggested order or an indent. |
| Specific result | A PO is created with the vendor, delivery date, items, quantities, and rates. |
| Clear end | The PO is sent for approval or sent to the vendor, depending on the required approval. |
| Observable completion | The PO and its status can be checked. |
| Done independently | One PO can be processed independently of other POs. |

**Conclusion:** T2 fits the task definition because it has a specific purpose, identifiable work, and a verifiable outcome. Entering vendor details, adding items, checking warnings, and requesting approval are steps or actions within the task—not separate tasks.

*Note: The Procurement Officer source lists raising, sending for approval, sending to the vendor, and cancelling as actions within T2. The exact completion boundary depends on the workflow and approval requirements.*

## Overall Conclusion

The same definitions can be applied across Credit Controller, Payroll Specialist, and Procurement Officer roles without modification.

- **Responsibility** describes the business result the role is accountable for.
- **Task** describes a distinct piece of work with an identifiable start and end, a specific result, and observable completion.
- **Steps** are the actions performed within a task; they do not automatically qualify as separate tasks.

These examples show that the definitions are applicable across different business functions. The actual ownership and boundaries of each task should still be validated with the relevant role holder.
